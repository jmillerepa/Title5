Module 1: Legal Hierarchy & "10+1" Shared Governance

Title 5 Code Sections: § 53200 – § 53206

1. Legal Hierarchy in California Higher Education

In California community colleges, legal authority flows down through three key levels:

  • California Education Code: Statutes passed by the State Legislature and signed by the Governor.
  • Title 5 (California Code of Regulations): Rules adopted by the California Community Colleges Board of Governors (BOG) to implement Ed Code.
  • Local Board Policies (BP) & Administrative Procedures (AP): District-level policies adopted by the local Board of Trustees (e.g., SMCCD BP 2.05).

2. The "10+1" Academic & Professional Matters (§ 53200)

Title 5 § 53200 guarantees local Academic Senates primary authority over 11 specific areas:

  • 1. Curriculum, including establishing prerequisites and placing courses within disciplines.
  • 2. Degree and certificate requirements.
  • 3. Grading policies.
  • 4. Educational program development.
  • 5. Standards or policies regarding student preparation and success.
  • 6. District and college governance structures, as related to faculty roles.
  • 7. Faculty roles and involvement in accreditation processes.
  • 8. Policies for faculty professional development activities.
  • 9. Processes for program review.
  • 10. Processes for institutional planning and budget development.
  • +1. Other academic and professional matters as mutually agreed upon.
SMCCD Application (BP 2.05): The District Board of Trustees must either "Rely Primarily Upon" the advice of the District Academic Senate (DAS) or reach "Mutual Agreement" depending on the specific item on the 10+1 list.

Module 2: Curriculum & Course Outline of Record (COR)

Title 5 Code Sections: § 55000 – § 55003

1. Course Outline of Record (COR) Standards (§ 55002)

The Course Outline of Record is the legal contract between the college, instructor, and student. Under Title 5 § 55002(a), every credit course COR must specify:

  • Unit value and total student learning hours.
  • Prerequisites, corequisites, or advisories (§ 55003).
  • Catalog description, measurable student learning outcomes, and course content.
  • Instructional methods and required out-of-class assignments.

2. Unit-to-Hour Calculation (§ 55002.5)

California standard attendance accounting requires 54 hours of total student learning for 1 unit of semester credit:

  • Lecture Unit: 18 hours of direct instruction + 36 hours of out-of-class study = 54 total hours.
  • Lab Unit: 54 hours of direct laboratory work with 0 out-of-class homework hours = 54 total hours.
SMCCD CurricUNET Process: At Cañada, CSM, and Skyline, local Curriculum Committees review CORs through CurricUNET to verify unit-to-hour math before sending courses to the SMCCD Board and state COCI database.

Module 3: Associate Degrees, GE & Transfer Mandates

Title 5 Code Sections: § 55060 – § 55064 | AB 928 & AB 1111 Integration

1. Title 5 Degree Standards (§ 55063)

To receive an Associate Degree in California, Title 5 mandates:

  • Minimum of 60 semester units of lower-division coursework.
  • Minimum cumulative GPA of 2.0 in all degree-applicable units.
  • At least 18 units in a major or area of emphasis.
  • Completion of General Education requirements and English/Math competencies.

2. CalGETC & Statewide Reforms (AB 928 / AB 1111)

Modern California legislation integrates directly into Title 5 operations across SMCCD:

  • AB 928 (CalGETC): Establishes a single, common general education pathway accepted by both CSU and UC systems, replacing duplicate transfer tracks.
  • AB 1111 (Common Course Numbering): Standardizes course numbers across all 116 California Community Colleges for seamless credit transfer.
  • ADTs (AA-T / AS-T): Associate Degrees for Transfer that guarantee admission with junior status to the CSU system.

Module 4: Student Services, DSPS & Academic Standards

Title 5 Code Sections: § 55021 – § 55046, § 56000+

1. Grading Symbols & Alleviating Substandard Grades (§ 55040–55045)

Title 5 standardizes evaluation symbols across the state:

  • Substandard Grades: D, F, NP (No Pass). Students are permitted up to 3 total attempts to alleviate substandard grades in a specific course.
  • Excused Withdrawal (EW): Awarded when a student must withdraw due to verifiable extenuating circumstances beyond their control.

2. DSPS Accommodations (5 CCR § 56027)

Disabled Students Programs and Services (DSPS) / Disability Resource Centers (DRC) in SMCCD enforce a 3-tiered accommodation process:

  • Level I: Standard Accommodations (extended test time, quiet environment, note-takers).
  • Level II: Course Substitutions (when standard accommodations are insufficient, evaluated without altering fundamental degree requirements).
  • Level III: Course Waivers (rare exceptions reviewed by District Academic Standards Committees).

Module 5: Faculty Qualifications & Equivalency Purview

Title 5 Code Sections: § 53400 – § 53430

1. Minimum Qualifications (MQs)

The California Community Colleges Board of Governors updates the official Disciplines List outlining minimum hiring qualifications:

  • Academic / Credit Disciplines: Master's degree in the discipline or related discipline.
  • CTE / Vocational Disciplines: Bachelor's degree + 2 years of industry experience OR Associate degree + 6 years of industry experience.

2. Equivalency Process (§ 53430)

If a candidate lacks the exact degree listed on the MQs handbook, Title 5 § 53430 mandates a formal process to grant Equivalency:

  • The candidate must prove qualifications that are at least equal to those required by the Disciplines List.
  • In SMCCD, the District Academic Senate (DAS) maintains the Equivalency Committee process to ensure peer faculty review.

Module 6: Distance Education, FTES & Compliance

Title 5 Code Sections: § 55200 – § 55210, § 58000+

1. Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI § 55204)

Title 5 § 55204 mandates that distance education courses maintain Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI) initiated by the instructor. Without verified RSI, distance education is legally classified as correspondence study, risking federal audit sanctions.

  • Substantive: Direct instruction, detailed feedback on coursework, leading discussions.
  • Regular: Predictable, scheduled opportunities for interaction throughout the term.

2. FTES & Attendance Accounting (§ 58000+)

Full-Time Equivalent Students (FTES) are the primary metric for state funding formulas:

  • 1 FTES: Equals 525 hours of student class contact time (equivalent to 1 student taking 15 units for two 17-week terms).
  • SMCCD Audits: Distance education courses at Cañada, CSM, and Skyline undergo peer Canvas shell evaluations to ensure DE compliance before census reporting.
